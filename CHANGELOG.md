# Changelog

All notable changes to Orphan Parts Lens. Versions follow SemVer.

## Unreleased

### Changed
- The demo set is built from the owner's own photos: three monitors (Dell U2419H, Dell 1800FP, a Sun flat panel), a ThinkPad T510, an older ThinkPad (type 9461), a spare laptop board, an EVGA GeForce 9800 GT, an Intel slot 1 processor, a TP-Link TL-PA2010 powerline adapter and the frappé maker's worn agitator, 19 photos under `demo/photos` with serial numbers, MAC addresses and keys masked and no metadata, and every model answer for them recorded, each photo's own segmentation and read included, so the hosted demo shows real items at no cost and `./dev eval` scores 14 of 14 expected values with no stub answers. The agitator search brings back four written listings: one documented candidate, two rejected on a rule, one that needs evidence. `./dev record-fixtures` takes the key from the shell as well as from `.env`, and a recording names the digest of its request `digest` rather than `key`, which secret scanners read as a credential (recordings that say `key` still load); the model's encrypted reasoning is kept empty in a recording and left out of its key.
- The item dialog is laid out in sections: the facts (photos, intent, availability, profile, the values to confirm, details, a separated part), Decide, what it is worth, prepare to sell or give away, measure and fit, and the picture last, each a card with its title; a bar of section links under the name stays in view and scrolls to each. The photos are a row of thumbnails in the flow instead of a strip that covered the fields under it; rows of fields are a grid with the label above each input and the button on their line; checkboxes and radios sit on the line with their text, the mode as one line (*Mode: Sell / Give away*); the demo switch, **History** and **Delete** (outlined in red, at the right) are the footer. The picture section no longer shows a field and a disabled button while pictures are off on the server, only a line saying so; when on, the *as a...* field is empty with an example use as its placeholder and a line under it says the drawing is an illustration, labeled as drawn, never evidence; the Decide row's link reads *See picture controls*.
- The forms on the pages share the item dialog's layout: the Inbox's *Send a capture from this computer*, *Read a label* and its save, and Build's preferences, proposal and outcome forms put the label above each control in a grid that drops to one column on a phone, show a file input as its button and file name with a help line under it (types, size), mark optional fields with a muted *(optional)*, and put the submit button on its own row at the left, with one gap between fields and a wider one between groups.
- The phone guide, step 1 of the README and the docs home, and the Android guide say the app is not on Google Play: the APK comes from the releases, signed with the build's development key, so Android asks to allow installs from an unknown source (per app under **Install unknown apps** on Android 8 and newer), a Play Protect scan from the Play Store checks it once installed, and an earlier release is uninstalled before the next one, since each release carries a fresh key.

### Added
- **Plan the search** for a missing item, on *Find a part* and in an item's *Measure and fit* section: one Muse Spark call (`POST /api/searches/{id}/plan`, operation `search-plan`) writes three to six phrasings, each a Marketplace search link with the top price that the owner opens in a new tab, one match pattern over listing text and the exclusion words. Marketplace has no public search API and its search box takes no regular expressions, so the app does not search it: the pattern runs on RE2 (`google-re2`), linear in the text, so no pattern can hold the backend; it is checked for the owner's sake (at most 300 characters, compiles under RE2, no lookarounds, backreferences, `\C`, comments or inline flags other than `(?i)`, counted repeats expanding to at most 400, never matching empty text), can be edited and tried on a line, and ranks the listings brought back, several pasted at once or as screenshots (one allowance hit each), as fits, maybe or does not fit with the matched span highlighted, free of model calls; a listing is kept to its first 4,000 characters and a search to 50 listings, the oldest unchecked dropped first; the asking price is the first one stated (several amounts either side of the limit leave it a maybe), with `k` for thousands, *free* only when no amount is given, `CA$` not read as US dollars and `$1 200` not guessed at; **Check fit** reads one listing into a candidate with the seller questions, once even when asked twice at once; **Plan again** keeps the pattern and words the owner saved and offers the new pattern beside them. See `docs/guides/search.md`.
- A **Profile** page in the web header and **What should I do with it?** on the item's Decide section. The profile rates skills *none*, *some*, *confident* or *pro* (ten listed, from software and electronics to plumbing and home repair, plus your own) beside the tools, effort, opening devices, budget, currency and radius; it is the preferences record, whose `skills` are now `{skill, level}` and whose plain names, stored or sent, read as *some*. The button asks Muse Spark once (`POST /api/items/{id}/scenarios`, low effort, refused to the demo visitor) for three cards written for that profile: keep and fix (likely problems, steps at your level, difficulty for you and why, parts, cost, time, or not worth fixing), reuse (two or three, naming what else you own) and sell or give away (with the last price guidance or a button to ask for one), and one recommendation with its reason; without a profile they are written for an average person and say so. **Choose this** records the choice and moves the focus to where it leads (`keep_fix` is new; it adds the repair steps to the item's notes while the item is as the cards saw it, whole steps left out when the notes would overflow, and on stale cards the button says it comes without them; a decision does not make the cards or the price guidance stale, and once the steps are in the card says so), and the cards are kept on the item, stale once it changes; a reuse card names the kinds you own, without the counts the brief gave. Preferences written by the demo loader do not count as a profile.
- The hosted deploy writes `PICTURE_ENABLED` into the Modal secret from the repository variable of that name (`PICTURE_ENABLED_HOSTED` for `./dev deploy hosted`), so the hosted demo can draw pictures with Muse Image; it defaults to `0` and a value other than `0` or `1` is refused before anything is written.
- `./dev live-check`: one real Model API call per operation the product makes (`segment`, `transcribe`, `extract`, `listing-text`, `draft`, `uses`, `propose`, `price`, and `picture` when named), through the product's own services on one item from the demo set, each line with the model that answered, PASS or FAIL, milliseconds, tokens and cost; a live run spends real credit, held under `--max-usd` (default 0.25; an operation whose ceiling, retries counted, is alone above it is named and skipped up front) and refused when the spend ledger has less headroom; it never records fixtures, `OPL_FAKE_API=1` (or true/yes/on) replays the recordings for nothing, and a value that is neither on nor off is refused. `--against local|hosted|URL` runs the proof over HTTP against a running backend: behind a gate, an extract without the code is answered from the recordings and a write is refused with 403, then a session with `LIVECHECK_ACCESS_CODE` (from the environment, never an argument; `./dev` passes it and the key on by name and reads neither) reads the live models and signs out. The check lives in `livecheck/` at the repository root, mounted read-only into the development backend container and in no image or deploy; `backend/tests/test_livecheck.py` pins the Modal app, the build contexts, the Dockerfiles and the production volumes and runs the dry run with a placeholder key that must not appear in the output, `scripts/tests/test_livecheck.sh` scans the folder for anything that reads like a credential, runs gitleaks over it when installed and allows only tracked Python and Markdown in it.
- A diagram of how Orphan Parts Lens works (`docs/assets/process.svg`, drawn as vector text and shapes): capture, the reading and the four paths with their outcomes, the preferences that shape proposals, and the private inventory; shown under the intro of the README and the docs home.
- **Sign out** in the web header while signed in on a gated host: `DELETE /api/access` clears the session cookie with the attributes it was set with (`HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`, `Secure` when the cookie is), the web app drops the token it keeps across origins even when the call fails, and the page loads again as the demo visitor, the footer's pairing code with it.
- A pictured guide to pairing the phone with the web app (`docs/start/pairing.md`): sign in, the footer's pairing code signed in and signed out, the scan, the typed address, a photo sent to the Inbox, and what a visitor gets with the code and without it; linked from the judges' guide, the README and the docs home.
- **Unpair** on the app's Backend tab, shown while the phone holds an address, a code or a session: after a confirmation that names the phone's own code by its label and where it is revoked, the app asks the backend to forget the session (`DELETE /api/access`, a short wait, any answer), empties the address, the code, its label and expiry and the token on disk, cancels a claim still running and shows the empty form. The phone's code stays issued until `./dev keys revoke "phone …"` on the server ends it.

### Fixed
- `./dev -d` (and `bash -x` over the deploy scripts) no longer prints values read from `.env` or secrets from the shell in its trace: `env_get`, `env_load` and `env_set`, the key check before `up`, `gate`, the live check's settings, the e2e access code, `deploy.sh`'s `.env` reader and the hosted secret step run with tracing off and turn it back on after. `scripts/tests/test_trace.sh` runs these paths traced against sentinel values that must appear nowhere in the output.
- `./dev deploy hosted backend` and the deploy workflow restart the deployed app's running containers after the deploy and wait until they are gone before asking `/readyz`, so a changed Modal secret applies; a warm container (`OPL_MIN_CONTAINERS=1`) kept the secret it started with until it was stopped by hand. The workflow installs `modal` 1.6.3 or later.
- The demo visitor no longer gets the owner's private text on a public item or capture: `GET /api/items/{id}` answered a public item's `location` and `notes` in full and the item dialog showed them. Wherever an item is answered to the demo visitor its `location` and `notes` are now blank and its voice notes (values with the source `voice_note`) are left out; a capture's `note` is blank and its clip and spoken note are reported as absent (`clip`, `audio` false, their sizes 0). This is done where the shapes are serialized, so every route the visitor reaches, and any added later, leaves them out; the owner, signed in with the code, still gets everything. An item served this way says so (`redacted: true`). The item dialog shows the visitor the details read-only, with no location or notes boxes and no **Save details**, and the same holds for any item that comes back redacted (a session that ended unnoticed). A save of the details now sends only the fields that were changed, so blanks the visitor was served are never written over the owner's values, even after a session opens in another tab. A capture read without a name or photos now names its item *Capture* instead of the first words of the phone's line, which stays in the notes.
- Text typed in the item's details and not saved yet is kept when another part of the dialog changes the item (a decision, a confirmed value, a picture); only the fields you did not touch take the saved values.
- A value no longer carries an *as printed* line that only repeats it in another letter case, spacing or punctuation (a label's *cULus* read as *CULUS*); the line stays when the label says more, such as a unit or a prefix.
- Live calls no longer stop with `response incomplete: max_output_tokens` before the answer: reasoning tokens count against `max_output_tokens` on the Responses API, and a label read at effort `low` spent 798 of its 800 on reasoning. A caller's `max_output_tokens` is now the answer budget alone; the client adds a headroom by effort (1,024 `minimal`, 4,096 `low`, 8,192 `medium`, 16,384 `high`, 32,768 `xhigh`), under `MODEL_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS` when set (`0`, the default, is no ceiling). Tokens are billed as used, so the larger cap costs nothing unless the model uses it. Fixtures stay keyed on the answer budget, so recordings still match. A response that still stops at the cap says which operation, how many output tokens were used and how many of them were reasoning.
- Price guidance no longer fails with `output did not match Findings: Invalid JSON ... at line 1 column 1`: with the search tool the model may write a line before the search ("Tracking your ... down") and the answer in the message after it, and the client read every message as one text. A typed answer is now read from the final message alone, for `respond_structured` and the tool loop alike; a final message that carries prose ahead of the object is read from the object it ends with; one with no object raises naming the operation and the checks that failed, with the text cut short.
- Price guidance is bounded to `PRICE_MAX_SEARCHES` web searches (4; 1 to 10), sent as `max_tool_calls` on the request and said in the instructions: left to itself a guidance ran eleven or twelve searches over 16 to 31 seconds, read 70,000 to 100,000 input tokens and cost 7 to 8 cents before the answer.

### Added
- A phone paired by QR gets an access code of its own: the QR of a signed-in page carries a pairing, a session of its own kind good for `PAIR_TOKEN_MINUTES` (15), and the app claims a code with it (`POST /api/pair/code`, once per pairing; a browser's session is refused, and each try counts against the address's code attempts), a record like any issued code, labeled `phone` and part of the pairing's id, good for `PAIR_CODE_DAYS` (30) and never past the code that paired it, and one code pairs at most `PAIR_CODE_MAX` (10) phones with current codes; the app then opens sessions with that code, with no second scan. Revoking a guest's code ends the phone codes it paired; a phone paired under the master code ends when `DEMO_ACCESS_CODE` changes; `./dev keys revoke "phone …"` ends that phone (schema v12 links a phone's record to what paired it). The pairing endpoints draw only an origin the server is reached at. `./dev keys list` shows the phone codes; the app's Backend tab names the code by label and expiry, and Check says which code opens the door. The footer's pairing code shows for the demo visitor too, with the address alone, so a phone paired from an anonymous page is the demo visitor as well.
- A QR code that opens the newest release's APK: each release attaches the APK a second time as `orphan-parts.apk`, so `https://github.com/nikolareljin/orphan-parts/releases/latest/download/orphan-parts.apk` is a fixed address; the judges' guide, the README and the docs home show the code (`docs/assets/apk-qr.svg`) with the link; a release is marked the latest only once its APK is attached, and only when it is the highest version.
- `./dev gate on|off|show`: the access code on your own machine, made when `.env` has none, the backend restarted with the gate, a code for the phone or a guest issued and both printed to copy; never part of a deployment. The scripts read `.env` as Compose does: a ` #` comment after a value, the text inside quotes, the last of a repeated key, Windows line endings.
- A picture of what an item could become: *What it could look like* on the item dialog, under the photos, with a field for the use and **Draw it**; Muse Image draws it only on that click (`POST /api/items/{id}/picture`), from the item's type, accepted values (never a private one such as a serial number, MAC or IMEI), condition and faults; kept beside the photos as `picture-<n>.jpg` and never counted as one, served by `GET /api/items/{id}/pictures/{n}`, captioned as drawn and never evidence, charged in the ledger per image; the demo visitor sees the pictures of public items and is refused a new one. Off unless `PICTURE_ENABLED` is set; the stub `images-generations/picture.json` answers fixture mode.

The picture row waits for its call.
- The evaluation loop: `./dev eval` (`python -m app.eval run`) loads the demo set as the demo does and scores it by the rules into `eval/` (a report as JSON and Markdown, two charts): values read per item against an `expect:` block in `demo/set.yaml`, fit verdicts between items, proposals written against kept, stub answers and spend; `export` writes `train.jsonl` from the accepted reads, `diff` compares two reports; the guide *Training and improving the reads* says where the rules and the prompts live and what fine-tuning Muse Spark Open would take.

### Fixed
- The deploy workflow installs `python-dotenv` next to `modal`: `modal secret create --from-dotenv` needs it, and the backend job stopped at the secret step without it; the install lines for the by-hand path name it too.
- The hosted backend serves no API docs: the secret step writes `API_DOCS=0`, and `/docs`, `/redoc` and `/openapi.json` answer 404 on the host; on a machine of your own they stay on (`API_DOCS`, default `1`).
- Generated pictures are decoded and stripped of metadata before storage; the decision panel links to the picture controls.
- The local gate checks labels before changing state; environment readers agree on duplicate values and quoted comments, and the setter refuses malformed assignments.
- Ideas keep the item version used for their brief when an edit happens during the request; decision rows fit a phone dialog.
- Material matching preserves uncertain claims, mixed wood or ceramic descriptions, and component or coating descriptions instead of approving the whole part as metal or plastic.
- The frappé maker's agitator in the demo set asks for a metal replacement: the material on the need side is the one wanted, the worn rod's plastic is a fault note, so a rod of the same dimensions that a listing calls all-metal or stainless steel passes and a plastic one is a conflict (a material is read as the one word metal or plastic, however it is spelled; a mix stays as written and does not pass).
- A material followed by punctuation or in parentheses ("Metal.", "(stainless)") reads as metal or plastic instead of a conflict, as do POM, nylon and "BPA-free plastic"; a negation ("non-metal", "no plastic", "plastic-free"), a part or a surface ("mostly metal", "chrome-plated") stays as written; the agitator's material question asks the seller whether the rod is metal throughout.
- A release merge whose tag the ruleset refuses now fails with the exact command an admin runs to tag the merge commit, in the run summary, instead of an opaque rule violation.

### Security
- No credential or personal data ships in an image: `.env` files and logs are kept out of both build contexts (and local data out of the backend's), and the release workflow scans each built image (`scripts/scan-image.sh`: its environment, the project's files, gitleaks) before anything is pushed.
- A hosted deploy never ships a local secret file: the Modal image leaves out `.env*`, key, certificate and keystore files and Python caches wherever they turn up under the three directories it carries, and the web step refuses to build while a `frontend/.env*` exists (Vite would bake its `VITE_` names into the public bundle) and refuses to upload a `dist/` holding one.

- Build pack and outcome: `POST /api/projects/{id}/pack` writes a Markdown build pack by the rules from what stands (goal, items with accepted values and never a serial, location or note, what is still needed with its search and documented candidates, costs with dates, tools, steps with progress, safety, unknowns, guidance, limits, the outcome), versioned with a digest of its inputs and marked `stale` when an item, the project or the budget changed; `GET /api/packs/{id}.md` as a file; `POST /api/projects/{id}/progress` ticks a step; `POST /api/projects/{id}/outcome` records what was set up, how it was tested, the day, whether it works and the limits, moving a working project to done and keeping its items reserved; `.../outcome/share` turns it on and gives a text for others with no names, ids, values, places or prices. On the Build and reuse page.
- Demo gate: with `DEMO_ACCESS_CODE` set, every page and API call needs a session opened with the code at `POST /api/access` (a cookie, or the token as a bearer header), signed with a key derived from the code and stored nowhere; so many tries per address; every call that spends is counted per session and hour (`DEMO_VISITOR_CALLS_PER_HOUR`) and for everyone per day (`DEMO_DAILY_CALLS`), over which the answer is 429 with `Retry-After`; every request has a per-address limit; `DEMO_PAUSED=1` refuses what spends; `/readyz` reports the gate under `demo`; behind nginx the forwarded address counts when `DEMO_TRUST_PROXY=1`. The web app asks for the code and again on any 401.
- Missing items: `POST /api/searches` records what an item needs (or values written in under a profile) with a top price, currency and radius, and hands off a Marketplace search for the owner to run; `POST /api/searches/{id}/candidates` takes a listing brought back as text (Muse Spark reads it into fields, with the profile's rule keys), as a screenshot (read by the extractor) or as typed values, dates it, assesses it by the profile's rules and names its state from the ladder; recheck, withdraw, and `GET /api/candidates/{id}/questions`, the rules' questions as a message to the seller. Nothing is fetched or posted; a link is kept as given. The Find a part page.
- Releases by hand and in full: `VERSION` is the one place the version is read from, and `./dev version` writes it with the manifests; `./dev release` tags `VERSION` on a clean, level `main` and pushes the tag, which only the repository admin may do; the release workflow assembles the release notes from the changelog section, the pull requests merged since the previous release, the compare link and the images, and carries two jobs that stay inert until there is something for them: a release APK when an Android app is in the tree, and a deploy to the host when the repository variable `DEPLOY_ENABLED` and the host secrets are set; `./dev deploy` does the same from a machine.
- Price guidance: `POST /api/items/{id}/price` has Muse Spark search the web (search grounding) for listings and sales of the same model and keeps only comparables a search result backs; four figures never merged (asking, sold, what you reported, the model's estimate) each with count, range, typical, source and date; three labelled strategies; weak evidence called weak; whole against parts from the parts' own guidance; `POST /api/items/{id}/price/reports` for a sale you know of; searches counted into the spend ledger. The What is it worth? panel on each item.
- Sell or give away: `POST /api/items/{id}/drafts` writes a listing draft with Muse Spark from the item's accepted values; the rules append the faults, what was tested, what is included and the price or free pickup, and drop any line that rests on nothing confirmed; a checklist before handover (preparation, photos, data removal when the item holds data) kept per draft; drafts versioned and marked stale when the item changes; copy the draft and open the Marketplace create page. `POST /api/items/{id}/voice` transcribes a WAV note with Muse Voice Transcribe and keeps it as the owner's words. The Prepare to sell or give away panel on each item.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-29

### Added
- Release on merge: a pull request from `release/x.y.z` merged into `main` tags the merge commit and publishes the images and the GitHub release, after the version is checked against every manifest and the changelog; an existing tag is never moved.
- Assessment: `POST /api/assess` checks a candidate against what is needed, rule by rule from the profile (`GET /api/profiles/{id}/rules`): pass, fail or unknown, never a guess; measured values carry their uncertainty into the check; one verdict from the ladder (`EXACT_MATCH`, `COMPATIBLE_LIKELY`, `VISUALLY_SIMILAR`, `CONFLICT`, `INSUFFICIENT_INFO`); a question for every unknown on the candidate's side. The Does it fit? panel on each item.
- Measurement: `POST /api/measure` and `POST /api/items/{id}/measurements` compute a dimension from marks on a photo with a scale reference (two points of known length, or four corners of a known rectangle through a homography), refuse captures that are blurred, short on reference, off the photo, too small or not straight on, and report the uncertainty with its basis; profile features with tolerances in `GET /api/profiles`; accepted captures are proposed on the item as observed values. The marking canvas under each item.
- Docs site: every page carries share metadata (title, description, one card image), so a link pasted into a chat or a post shows the project card.
- Private inventory: `POST /api/items` saves a reading as an item whose observed values are confirmed or rejected one by one; owner values sit beside observations with their provenance; condition, faults, tests, accessories, location, notes, intent and availability; parts separated into their own records; versions, optimistic writes and a per-item history; SQLite under `DATA_DIR`. The My inventory page and Save privately in the web app.
